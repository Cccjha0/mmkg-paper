# Introduction Draft

Knowledge graph completion (KGC) aims to infer missing facts from observed triples and has become a standard way to improve knowledge graph quality. In multimodal knowledge graph completion (MMKGC), entities may be associated with auxiliary modalities such as text and images, making it possible to enrich entity representations beyond pure graph structure. In product-centered knowledge graphs, this promise is especially appealing because many relations, such as style, color, or design details, appear intuitively compatible with visual evidence. A natural expectation is therefore that multimodal models should outperform structure-only approaches once image and text features are incorporated into link prediction.

However, this expectation becomes much less straightforward under realistic incomplete-modality conditions. In practical product graphs, visual support is often missing, unevenly distributed, or only useful for a subset of prediction settings. As a result, the question is no longer simply how to fuse more modalities, but when multimodal information actually provides useful signal and when the model falls back to structural regularities. This issue is particularly important for OpenBG-IMG, where the current paper protocol exhibits a strong asymmetry between prediction target position and image availability: head-side targets can still be image-supported, whereas tail-side targets are effectively image-missing. Under such a setting, multimodal gain may be real without being globally dominant.

Our empirical results make this tension explicit. Under a unified OpenBG-IMG protocol with filtered ranking, `direction=both`, and 3-seed reporting, the strongest overall model is not the most complete multimodal architecture. Instead, `Residual-only` achieves the best global performance, followed by `ComplEx`, while the `Full Model` consistently improves over simpler multimodal baselines such as `Gate-only` and `Early Fusion` but still fails to surpass the stronger structural alternatives overall. This means that the central empirical fact of the current study is not global multimodal superiority, but the coexistence of two observations: multimodal fusion is useful, yet structure-dominant compensation remains globally stronger.

This tension motivates the main question of this paper: when does multimodal information help MMKGC under missing-visual conditions, and why does that help remain bounded under the current protocol? To answer this question, we study the problem from four complementary perspectives. First, we perform subgroup analysis based on target-side image availability and show that the clearest multimodal-favorable regime appears in `head_has_img`, while overall performance is still dominated by the structurally stronger `tail_noimg` regime. Second, we conduct relation-type analysis and show that the `Full Model` outperforms `Gate-only` on most medium-support and high-support relations, but still surpasses `Residual-only` on only a minority of retained relations. Third, we analyze model behavior through gate statistics, residual contribution, and branch-preference dynamics, showing that fusion remains useful but the final representation is persistently biased toward the residual path. Finally, we support these findings with case studies, where successful multimodal cases concentrate in `head + has_img` settings, while structure-favorable failures concentrate in `tail + no_img` settings.

Based on these results, we argue that multimodal gain in OpenBG-IMG is real but bounded. Its usefulness depends on target position, modality availability, relation characteristics, and the competition between fusion and structural compensation. Consequently, this paper should not be read as proposing a globally superior multimodal architecture. Rather, its main contribution is a gain-boundary analysis: we show when multimodal information helps, why those gains remain local, and why stronger structural compensation continues to dominate under the current protocol.

In summary, this paper makes the following contributions:

1. We provide a unified empirical comparison of structural baselines, fusion-based multimodal models, residual-only modeling, and a full multimodal architecture on OpenBG-IMG.
2. We show that multimodal gain is not globally uniform, but instead bounded by target position, image availability, and relation characteristics.
3. We explain this boundary through behavior analysis, showing that gate adaptation is relation-aware but not dominant, while final branch preference remains residual-dominant.
4. We validate the boundary pattern at the sample level through success and failure case studies that match the subgroup and behavior evidence.
