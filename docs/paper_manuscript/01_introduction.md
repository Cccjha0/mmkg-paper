# Introduction

Knowledge graph completion (KGC) aims to infer missing facts from observed triples and remains a central task for improving knowledge graph quality. In multimodal knowledge graph completion (MMKGC), entities may additionally be associated with auxiliary modalities such as text and images, making it possible to enrich entity representations beyond pure graph structure. In product-oriented knowledge graphs, this possibility is especially appealing because many relations appear intuitively compatible with visual evidence. A natural expectation is therefore that once text and image signals are incorporated into link prediction, multimodal models should outperform structure-only alternatives.

Under incomplete visual support, however, this expectation becomes much less straightforward. In realistic product graphs, image availability is uneven, sometimes missing, and not equally useful across prediction settings. As a result, the key question is no longer simply how to fuse more modalities, but when multimodal information actually provides useful signal and when stronger structural regularities remain more reliable. This issue is particularly important in OpenBG-IMG under the current paper protocol, where prediction target position is entangled with modality availability: head-side targets can still be image-supported, whereas tail-side targets are effectively image-unavailable. Under such a setting, multimodal gain may be real without being globally dominant.

Our earlier analysis made this tension explicit. Under a unified OpenBG-IMG protocol with filtered ranking, `direction=both`, and three-seed reporting, the strongest overall model is not the most complete multimodal architecture. Instead, `Residual-only` achieves the best global performance, followed by `ComplEx`, while the `Full Model` consistently improves over weaker multimodal baselines such as `Gate-only` and `Early Fusion` but still fails to surpass the stronger structural alternatives overall. Further subgroup and relation-type analysis show that multimodal gain is not uniformly available across the task. It appears most clearly in image-supported head-side conditions, remains limited in structure-favorable regimes, and is shaped jointly by target position, modality availability, relation characteristics, and branch competition.

These findings substantially narrow the problem. The central empirical issue is no longer whether multimodal information can ever help, but whether it should be activated for the current query. If multimodal gain is conditional rather than globally reliable, then always-on fusion is no longer the most natural strategy. A more appropriate response is to treat multimodal activation itself as a decision problem: preserve multimodal evidence where it is likely to help, and fall back to a stronger structural expert where fusion is expected to be unhelpful or even harmful.

Motivated by this observation, this paper moves from protocol-aware gain-boundary analysis to **gain-aware selective activation**. We propose a lightweight **gain-threshold routing** framework that predicts whether multimodal fusion is worth activating for a given query and then selects between two fixed experts through hard-threshold routing. Concretely, we use `Gate-only` as a fusion expert and `Residual-only` as a structural expert, construct query-level gain labels from development-set reciprocal-rank improvement, and train protocol-aware routers that combine subgroup, relation-prior, modality-consistency, and expert-confidence features. The resulting routing framework does not attempt to build a universally stronger multimodal architecture. Instead, it operationalizes the earlier analytical finding by converting bounded multimodal gain into a query-level selective activation mechanism.

Experiments show that this reformulation is effective under the current OpenBG-IMG protocol. Using the official model-comparison line, `Residual-only` remains the strongest fixed model overall, while `Full Model` remains the strongest multimodal variant in the internal family. Using a unified query-level recomputed line for routing experiments, however, the best learned router (`XGBoost + delta=0.01 + tau=0.7`) reaches an overall MRR of `0.3160`, outperforming the fixed `Residual-only` expert (`0.2930`), the original `Full Model` (`0.2100`), and the rule-based router, while still leaving a gap to Oracle routing. Subgroup, threshold-scan, and feature-ablation results further show that the learned router does not reduce to a shallow “has image -> use fusion” rule. Instead, it becomes more conservative in structure-favorable conditions and uses multimodal fusion selectively where local gain is more plausible.

This paper therefore makes a deliberately controlled claim. We do not argue that multimodal fusion is globally superior or that structural modeling has become secondary. On the contrary, our results reinforce that stronger structural compensation remains indispensable under the current protocol. What changes is that multimodal evidence no longer needs to compete globally in an always-on manner. Once bounded gain is reformulated as a routing problem, multimodal information can be activated selectively in the conditions where it is expected to help, while structural fallback remains responsible for the large structure-favorable region of the test space.

In summary, this paper makes the following contributions:

1. We establish a protocol-aware problem framing in which multimodal gain on OpenBG-IMG is bounded, conditional, and entangled with target position, image availability, and relation context rather than globally uniform.
2. We reformulate this bounded-gain phenomenon as a **query-level selective activation problem** and propose a lightweight **gain-threshold routing** framework that chooses between a fusion expert (`Gate-only`) and a structural expert (`Residual-only`) through hard-threshold decision making.
3. We design a protocol-aware router feature space that combines target regime, relation-level gain priors, modality-consistency signals, and expert-confidence cues, and show through feature ablation that the learned router does not collapse into a trivial image-availability heuristic.
4. We provide empirical evidence that selective activation is effective under the current protocol: the best learned router outperforms the strongest fixed expert and the original `Full Model` on the routing evaluation line, while Oracle routing indicates additional headroom for future work.
