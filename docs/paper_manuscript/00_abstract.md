# Abstract

Multimodal knowledge graph completion (MMKGC) augments link prediction with auxiliary modalities such as text and images. In product-oriented knowledge graphs, this setting is especially appealing because many relations appear visually grounded. Under incomplete visual support, however, multimodal benefit is not uniformly available. Under the current OpenBG-IMG protocol, this issue is further complicated by protocol-shaped role--modality asymmetry: head-side targets may still be image-supported, whereas tail-side targets are effectively image-unavailable. The resulting problem is therefore not only whether multimodal information helps, but also how multimodal gain should be activated under an asymmetric missing-modality protocol.

We study this problem on OpenBG-IMG under a unified protocol with filtered ranking, `direction=both`, and three-seed reporting. Protocol-aware subgroup and relation analyses show that multimodal gain is real but bounded: it is concentrated in specific local regimes rather than globally reliable across the benchmark. Under a clean query-time legality constraint, a naive global-threshold learned router does not outperform a simple legal rule, showing that deployable routing cannot be characterized adequately by a single global decision boundary.

We therefore move beyond the naive global-threshold formulation and evaluate stronger clean routing designs. Direction-specific thresholding consistently improves over the clean rule baseline, showing that the clean decision boundary is highly asymmetric across query directions. More target-aligned supervision further strengthens clean routing: regression-based gain prediction yields the strongest clean result, improving over both the clean rule and `Residual-only`, with statistically supported gains. These findings show that the weakness of clean routing is not solely due to insufficient query-time observable signals. It also arises from overly coarse policy design and binary gain supervision.

At the same time, the best clean strategy still remains substantially below Oracle routing. The contribution of this work is therefore not a universally dominant multimodal architecture, but a protocol-aware transition from bounded-gain diagnosis to stronger structured clean routing. Under the current OpenBG-IMG protocol, deployable multimodal gain can be recovered more effectively once the routing policy is structured appropriately and trained with a more target-aligned objective, although a substantial deployable gap still remains.
