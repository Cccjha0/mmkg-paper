# Abstract

Multimodal knowledge graph completion (MMKGC) augments link prediction with auxiliary modalities such as text and images. In product-oriented knowledge graphs, this setting is especially appealing because many relations appear visually grounded. Under incomplete visual support, however, multimodal benefit is not uniformly available. This raises a more precise question: not only whether multimodal information helps, but when it should be activated under protocol-shaped missing-modality conditions.

We study this problem on OpenBG-IMG under a unified protocol with filtered ranking, `direction=both`, and three-seed reporting. Under the official model-comparison line, the strongest fixed model is not the most complete multimodal architecture: `Residual-only` is the best overall model, while `Full Model` remains only the strongest multimodal variant within the internal family. Protocol-aware subgroup, relation-group, and behavior evidence jointly show that multimodal gain is real but bounded. It is strongest in image-supported head-side regimes, weaker in structure-favorable conditions, and shaped by target position, modality availability, relation context, and residual-dominant branch competition.

Motivated by this bounded-gain structure, we reformulate the problem from always-on fusion to query-level selective activation and propose a lightweight gain-threshold routing framework. The method uses `Gate-only` as a fusion expert, `Residual-only` as a structural expert, and a protocol-aware router trained from development-side query-level gain labels. Under a unified query-level routing line, the best learned router (`XGBoost + delta=0.01 + tau=0.7`) reaches an overall MRR of `0.3160`, outperforming the fixed `Residual-only` expert (`0.2930`), the rule-based router, and the original always-on `Full Model`, while still leaving room to Oracle routing.

These results support a controlled conclusion. The contribution of this work is not a universally dominant multimodal architecture, but a protocol-aware transition from gain-boundary analysis to gain-aware selective activation. Under the current OpenBG-IMG protocol, multimodal gain is conditional rather than globally reliable, and a lightweight router can exploit this bounded-gain structure more effectively than always-on multimodal fusion.
