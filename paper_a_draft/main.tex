\begin{abstract}
Static score fusion can exploit complementarity between frozen multimodal knowledge graph completion models. Query-dependent adjustment is an option for responding to query heterogeneity, but can degrade an effective static reference. Anchored Dynamic Correction (ADC) maps a learned preference to a bounded adjustment around a development-selected Global weight, with a margin-strength gate whose benefit is pair-dependent. Its action bound gives no reciprocal-rank risk guarantee. On MKG-W and DB15K, with three paired base seeds, we evaluate four primary M-Hyper pairs and two additional architecture pairs. ADC gains 0.02--0.24 MRR percentage points over Global in three primary pairs, with conditional bootstrap intervals above zero. The remaining primary effect is slightly negative; its interval and both additional-pair intervals span zero. Healthy DynaSemble has higher MRR than ADC in two primary pairs, and Relation also exceeds ADC on MKG-W with M-Hyper + NativE (W-N). ADC has lower unconditional mean reciprocal-rank loss relative to Global than healthy DynaSemble in all four primary pairs; Global itself has zero reference-relative loss. Matched hard-clipped correction also has slightly higher MRR in all four primary pairs. A 14-parameter selector still requires both scorers: historical pre-repair total time was 6.2--11.8 times primary-only, and corrected timing is unavailable. Both datasets had been inspected during development, so this is a retrospective evaluation of general score post-processing in MMKGC. The evidence describes utility--loss and deployment trade-offs, without establishing independent confirmation, uniform superiority or end-to-end efficiency.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} multimodal knowledge graph completion; model combination; dynamic score correction; negative transfer; selective adaptation.

\section{Introduction}

Multimodal knowledge graph completion (MMKGC) predicts missing facts using graph structure and entity-associated modalities. We study the setting in which two independently trained scoring models are already available for the same queries and candidate entities, for example when different architectures have been retained from earlier model development. Freezing those models lets us test score-level post-processing without changing their representations or repeating base-model training. The question is whether their existing scores can be combined usefully; running both scorers still incurs their inference cost.

M-Hyper, NativE and AdaMF-MAT process modality information through different architectures~\citep{mhyper,native,adamf}. Together with uneven image/text coverage in our inputs (Table~\ref{tab:data-modalities}), this motivates studying their heterogeneous rankings. These observations do not establish the cause of their accuracy differences or make complementarity uniquely multimodal. Our post-processing uses candidate-score distributions and direction, while known-entity coverage and relation diagnostics characterize the MMKGC application setting.

A globally weaker model can still contribute useful rankings. For example, on MKG-W a development-selected static mixture of M-Hyper and NativE outperforms either standalone scorer (Table~\ref{tab:claims-main}). This establishes useful static combination for that pair, not that query-dependent weights can be learned from the available features or improve on Global. We therefore measure the benefit and loss of any subsequent adjustment relative to Global.

Dynamic weighting can instead lose performance relative to an effective static mixture. Predicting which standalone model has the better rank supplies a convenient supervised signal, but converting that prediction directly into a mixture weight can increase the influence of a weaker model. In our four primary evaluation pairs, direct sigmoid-score weighting improves on the DEV-selected Global baseline in one pair and degrades in three. Global is the pure primary endpoint in three of those pairs. We therefore ask whether a reference-relative correction improves the utility--loss trade-off, allowing that no adjustment may be preferable. The argument proceeds from observed static complementarity, through possible failure of dynamic weighting, to an empirical test of reference-relative correction; it does not presume a dynamic gain.

The standalone-expert Oracle does not close this gap. It picks the better endpoint after observing the gold answer, whereas a deployable policy must infer one weight from observable query information. Different answers to the same observable query can favor different endpoints, but the policy must use the same weight for that query. Moreover, endpoint-winner labels omit the magnitude of RR changes and their value relative to Global. Finite DEV supervision, limited score-summary features and the chosen action map leave estimation and policy restrictions between Oracle opportunity and realized utility. Neither Oracle potential nor a positive static gain proves that a learned policy can exploit complementarity (Section~\ref{sec:complementarity}).

We address this problem with \emph{Anchored Dynamic Correction} (\adc). The method first selects a static reference weight on the development split (DEV). A logistic model then summarizes query-level score geometry into a model-preference signal. Instead of treating that signal as the final mixture weight, \adc\ maps it to a bounded offset from the reference and gates adjustment by preference strength to retain the reference on selected queries. This construction separates the choice of a reliable starting solution from the amount of query-specific adjustment. Dynamic ensembling and score-distribution inputs are established by prior work; our object of study is the reference-relative action map, its radius and its adjustment-rejection semantics, as compared in Table~\ref{tab:nearest-methods}. We do not claim that tanh, clipping, shrinkage or rejection is individually new.
We use \emph{conservative} for this testable reference-and-radius construction. It does not mean that few queries change or that reciprocal-rank losses are unlikely. Those outcomes require separate empirical comparisons against named policies and risk measures; Global itself has zero reference-relative harm.

Our experiments evaluate both net utility and harmful corrections, retaining four primary and two additional architecture pairs. MKG-W supplies the primary retrospective evaluation and DB15K a secondary external evaluation, also retrospective. External denotes another dataset, not independence from prior method development. Three of four primary ADC-minus-Global effects have conditional intervals above zero; D-N is slightly negative and both additional-pair intervals span zero. Complete seed/direction results accompany the paired effects. Supplementary studies preserve failed DynaSemble transfers, add healthy training controls, and compare six action families sharing each fitted preference object and 41 DEV configurations. Hard-clipped logit correction slightly exceeds tanh ADC's TEST MRR in every primary pair, while utility--loss trade-offs vary. These studies follow historical TEST inspection and do not constitute fresh blind confirmation. The evidence motivates reference-relative action control but does not identify tanh as uniquely beneficial or establish that the gains justify dual-scorer deployment cost.

This paper makes three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Diagnosing opportunity versus realized utility.} Six-pair endpoint-winner and Oracle-gap statistics show complementarity, while winner and gold-rank strata link that opportunity to actual ADC gains and losses. Positive Oracle gaps coexist with near-zero or negative correction utility (Tables~\ref{tab:complement-pairs}, \ref{tab:complement-winners} and~\ref{tab:complement-rank_bins}); they do not prove learnable dynamic gains.
\item \textbf{Specifying reference-relative action control.} ADC makes the static reference, permitted displacement and rejection of an adjustment explicit. Equations~\eqref{eq:fallback} and~\eqref{eq:bound} establish exact fallback and bounded score perturbation under the stated grid condition, without an RR-risk guarantee. Table~\ref{tab:nearest-methods} locates this construction among existing dynamic, shrinkage and rejection mechanisms.
\item \textbf{Identifying conditional value and component trade-offs.} Shared-fit, matched-budget DEV/TEST comparisons show that simple clipping can exceed tanh ADC's MRR (Tables~\ref{tab:matched-dev} and~\ref{tab:matched-test}). Frozen radius/fallback counterfactuals locate component-specific gains and sacrifices (Table~\ref{tab:rerun-ablation}); comparisons with healthy dynamic controls expose the utility--loss trade-off (Tables~\ref{tab:claims-main} and~\ref{tab:conservative-fair}). This supports conditional action design, not a uniquely effective tanh map or a universal winner.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Frozen-model fusion and static references.}
Combining existing predictions is an established alternative to changing the underlying representations. Stacked generalization learns a combiner from predictions on examples held out of base-model fitting~\citep{wolpert1992}. In MMKGC, MoSE combines modality-split scores through equal averaging, relation-level boosting or an instance-specific meta-learner~\citep{mose}, so score fusion need not be query-dependent. Our pairs instead comprise independently trained M-Hyper, NativE and AdaMF-MAT scorers; their different fusion and augmentation designs motivate the pair choices, while their representations remain frozen~\citep{mhyper,native,adamf}.

An estimated combiner also needs comparison with a simple reference. In forecast combination, partially egalitarian LASSO selects forecasts and shrinks surviving weights toward equality~\citep{diebold_shin2019}. That work concerns regularized forecast weights, rather than per-query KGC corrections, but establishes shrinkage toward simple combinations as prior art. Our DEV-selected Global need not be an equal mixture. The direct alternative $\alpha=(1-\lambda)\alpha_0+\lambda p_A$ shrinks a learned query weight toward that reference and includes Global at $\lambda=0$. It is therefore a substantive baseline, not a novel ingredient of ADC; matched comparisons test whether a signed bounded correction adds value beyond it (Section~\ref{sec:matched-results}).

\paragraph{Query weights and preference calibration.}
DynaSemble learns query-dependent weights from candidate-score distributions of separately trained textual and structural KGC models~\citep{dynasemble}. Dynamic ensembling and these inputs precede ADC. Table~\ref{tab:nearest-methods} compares the supervision, reference, action space and rejection semantics of the nearest methods. DynaSemble's fixed coefficient is not a radius around a DEV-selected mixture, and MoSE's inference rules do not define a return-to-static confidence gate; numerical rankings can nevertheless coincide with static or single-model rankings. MoSE is a literature comparison here, with no new performance run. Healthy DynaSemble controls test dynamic competition, while shared-fit Query-soft, shrinkage and clipped maps isolate action design from input and learner changes.

Probability calibration asks whether reported confidence agrees with the frequency of the event being predicted~\citep{guo2017}. Here that event is the standalone endpoint winner, not a beneficial adjustment relative to Global. Even a calibrated winner probability would omit the magnitude and sign of the proposed correction's RR change. ADC's class-balanced logistic output is used as a preference signal; neither it nor $|2p_A-1|$ is validated as a probability of harmless adjustment. The threshold is selected for DEV MRR, not calibrated to a risk tolerance.
\input{tables/complementarity/nearest.tex}

\paragraph{Rejecting adjustments and defining risk.}
SelectiveNet jointly learns prediction and rejection, assessing risk on the covered domain~\citep{selectivenet}. ADC's fallback still returns the reference ranking for every rejected adjustment, so adjustment frequency is not prediction coverage. Shrink-gate and Clip-g share this gate. We assess RR utility and loss over all queries relative to Global, plus harm frequency and conditional loss; the action bound supplies no probability guarantee (Section~\ref{sec:conservative-definition}).

Evaluation must also separate fitting, selection and reporting. Cawley and Talbot show that optimizing a finite-sample selection criterion can itself overfit and bias performance comparisons~\citep{cawley2010}. Our grouped held-out DEV comparisons separate combiner fitting and tuning from fold assessment, and the feature/filter boundary separates observable inputs from answer-aware evaluation. These controls answer different questions from historical TEST independence: the recorded design history supports retrospective comparisons, not fresh confirmation (Appendices~\ref{app:information-boundary} and~\ref{app:test-history}).

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

Let a knowledge graph contain triples $(h,r,t)$ over entities $\mathcal E$ and relations $\mathcal R$, with modality features attached to entities. A tail query is $(h,r,?)$ and a head query is $(?,r,t)$. Two independently trained, frozen scoring models produce $s_p(q,e)$ and $s_s(q,e)$ for each candidate $e\in\mathcal E$. The subscripts denote the primary and secondary models. We orient all scores so that a larger score indicates greater plausibility.

\paragraph{Primary selection.}
We select the primary using standalone DEV MRR. The evaluated \emph{reliable-primary} regime requires a positive pooled primary-minus-secondary DEV difference, a positive difference in each of three paired seeds, and a positive lower bound of an original-triple clustered bootstrap 95\% interval. All six evaluated MKG-W/DB15K pairs meet these criteria. This definition establishes the empirical reliability asymmetry used to organize the experiments.

\paragraph{Score normalization and combination.}
Let $\widetilde s_p$ and $\widetilde s_s$ be query-wise standardized candidate scores. Each model uses its own unfiltered finite set $F_m(q)=\{e\in\mathcal E:s_m(q,e)\text{ is finite}\}$; entity IDs align, but these sets need not coincide. For $n_m=|F_m(q)|>0$, define $\mu_m=n_m^{-1}\sum_{e\in F_m}s_m(q,e)$ and the population standard deviation $\sigma_m=[n_m^{-1}\sum_{e\in F_m}(s_m(q,e)-\mu_m)^2]^{1/2}$. For $e\in F_m$, the transformation is
\begin{equation}
\widetilde s_m(q,e)=\frac{s_m(q,e)-\mu_m(q)}{\sigma_m(q)+\epsilon},
\qquad \epsilon=10^{-8},
\label{eq:zscore}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon=10^{-8}$ is added after the square root, not inside the variance or as a lower clamp. Non-finite entries become $-\infty$; a constant finite support becomes zero on that support, and an empty support becomes an all-$-\infty$ row. Neither the designated gold nor the evaluation fact index enters these statistics. Known-answer filtering is applied only to evaluation copies after normalization; exact standalone endpoint ranks are preserved. Appendix~\ref{app:normalization} gives the pseudocode, floating-point and abnormal-row limitations. We define a mixture by
\begin{equation}
s_\alpha(q,e)=\alpha\widetilde s_p(q,e)+(1-\alpha)\widetilde s_s(q,e),
\quad \alpha\in[0,1].
\label{eq:mixture}
\end{equation}
Throughout the paper, $\alpha$ is the \emph{primary weight}. Operationally, an exact $\alpha=1$ or 0 uses the raw standalone primary or secondary score vector, ignoring the inactive expert; interior actions use Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixture}. In labeled evaluation this dispatch preserves the corresponding raw standalone gold rank. Equality between raw and normalized endpoint ranks requires qualification under floating-point arithmetic (Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-contract}).

\paragraph{Reference-relative utility.}
Given a static reference $\alpha_0$, we assess a dynamic policy by its query-level reciprocal-rank change
\begin{equation}
\Delta_i=\RR_{\mathrm{policy},i}-\RR_{\alpha_0,i}.
\label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
An observation $i$ identifies an original triple, a paired model seed, and a prediction direction. A positive mean $\Delta_i$ indicates useful adaptation; a negative individual $\Delta_i$ is a harmful correction. This formulation makes average utility and the incidence of harm separate experimental quantities.

\section{Anchored Dynamic Correction}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{DEV-selected static reference}
\adc\ begins with a Global weight selected by exact filtered DEV MRR:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_0\in\arg\max_{\alpha\in\Agrid}\MRR_{\mathrm{DEV}}(s_\alpha),
\qquad \Agrid=\{0,0.05,\ldots,1\}.
\label{eq:anchor}
\end{equation}
If computed DEV MRR ties exactly, Global prefers the weight closest to 0.5, then the smaller weight; no MRR tolerance is used. Equivalently, it maximizes $(\MRR,-|\alpha-0.5|,-\alpha)$. The same rule applies within OOF training folds. The reference can be an interior mixture or a standalone endpoint. The policy retains it whenever adaptation is rejected, and all changes are expressed relative to it. Figure~\ref{fig:method} summarizes the pipeline.

\subsection{A winner-supervised preference signal}
\label{sec:signal}
We describe each query with 13 score-geometry features: prediction direction; each model's top-1 score, top-5 mean, top-1/top-2 margin, and score standard deviation; and the four corresponding primary-minus-secondary differences. These statistics summarize the concentration and disagreement of the models' candidate distributions. In the corrected protocol, feature extraction accepts only the two unfiltered score matrices and the prediction direction; it has no gold, reference-score, or filter-index input.

On DEV, we construct the binary target
\begin{equation}
y_i=\ind[\RR_{p,i}>\RR_{s,i}],
\label{eq:label}
\end{equation}
excluding equal-RR observations from classifier fitting. They remain in anchor/action selection and in DEV/TEST policy evaluation. Serving applies the same feature-only policy to every valid query: endpoint gold RR equality is unavailable then and is not a rejection condition. Appendix~\ref{app:ties-harm} reports the excluded fraction and deployed outcomes within ties, including every seed/direction cell. For each pair, we pool the three paired base seeds and both prediction directions into one training set and fit one shared selector, including its median imputer and standard scaler, on the non-tied rows. Seed identity is not a feature. A balanced logistic regression supplies the preference signal. With transformed features $x(q)$, the signed margin and sigmoid preference score are
\begin{equation}
g(q)=w^\top x(q)+b,
\qquad p_A(q)=\sigma(g(q)).
\label{eq:signal}
\end{equation}
The classifier has 13 coefficients and one intercept: 14 trainable selector parameters, excluding preprocessing state and both frozen scorers. The four difference coordinates are linear combinations of the eight expert statistics on complete inputs in exact arithmetic, so these are not 13 independent signals. They change the effective L2 regularization geometry after scaling, rather than expanding the affine prediction space; independent imputation can break the dependency (Appendix~\ref{app:feature-dimension}). This count does not measure feature-extraction or end-to-end cost. Its training target represents standalone model preference; the policy mapping determines how that preference changes the reference mixture. DEV answers supply supervision, while inference uses candidate-distribution statistics and direction.

Class balancing changes the fitted loss, so $p_A$ is not automatically the natural probability that the primary wins. Ties are also excluded from fitting. We use $g$ as a signed preference margin and $p_A=\sigma(g)$ as its bounded re-expression; ``confidence'' below denotes margin strength, not a win or safety probability. Even correct winner preference need not identify a profitable correction direction or magnitude. Appendix~\ref{app:winner-signal} quantifies this mismatch and compares balanced with unweighted logistic fitting on identical grouped DEV data.

\subsection{Bounded adjustment and fallback}
On valid queries, the preference signal produces the raw bounded proposal
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{\mathrm{raw}}(q)=\clip\!\left(\alpha_0+\beta\tanh(g(q)),0,1\right),
\label{eq:raw}
\end{equation}
where $\beta$ controls the radius of adjustment around the reference. Before predicting, the guarded implementation rejects non-finite feature rows; it also checks transformed features before the classifier, logits before sigmoid-score prediction, and the returned score before computing an action. Let $v(q)$ indicate that these checks pass. Only valid rows have a gate-strength score, termed confidence $c(q)=|2p_A(q)-1|$:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{\mathrm{cont}}(q)=
\begin{cases}
\alpha_0, & \neg v(q),\\
\alpha_0, & v(q)\ \text{and}\ c(q)<\tau,\\
\alpha_{\mathrm{raw}}(q), & v(q)\ \text{and}\ c(q)\geq\tau.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:fallback}
\end{equation}
For finite logits, the gate and proposed magnitude use the same signal:
\begin{equation}
c=|\tanh(g/2)|,\qquad |\beta\tanh(g)|=\beta\frac{2c}{1+c^2}.
\label{eq:confidence-displacement}
\end{equation}
Thus a retained query with $c\geq\tau$ has an unclipped proposed displacement of at least $2\beta\tau/(1+\tau^2)$. Increasing $\tau$ selects larger absolute logits; it does not rescale the proposal of a query retained under both thresholds. Confidence selection and proposed magnitude are therefore coupled. Clipping can reduce actual displacement, including to zero, and grid projection further changes it; the formula is not a general lower bound on realized weight movement or ranking benefit.

The final applied weight is $\widehat\alpha(q)=\Pi_{\Agrid}(\alpha_{\mathrm{cont}}(q))$. Projection selects the nearest grid value, breaking equal distances first toward the anchor and then toward the smaller weight. The 0.05 grid is part of the reported algorithm: around a grid-aligned anchor it suppresses proposals within approximately 0.025, adding an action dead zone beyond the confidence gate. It also permits offline reuse of the gold answer's exact full-entity rank at each grid weight for labeled DEV selection and fixed-policy evaluation. Those cached ranks are not inference inputs. Projection changes the magnitude of surviving adjustments as well, so its ranking effect requires measurement. The fixed sensitivity comparison in Appendix~\ref{app:grid-sensitivity} retains three positive and one negative primary TEST effect relative to Global under finer and continuous weights, while revealing resolution-dependent utility and loss.

The original DEV search uses $\beta\in\{0.05,0.10,\ldots,0.50\}$ and $\tau\in\{0,0.1,0.2,0.3\}$. The selected pair maximizes DEV MRR; ties favor smaller $\beta$ and then larger $\tau$. The model, preprocessing, reference, grids, and selected parameters are serialized before TEST application. The matched supplement adds a single explicit $\beta=0$ policy, called ADC + Global, which returns the reference for every query regardless of confidence. This extends the original 40 configurations to 41; the original results remain identifiable. Algorithm~\ref{alg:policy} gives the inference procedure.

For a new query, both frozen experts supply scores, but only the selected mixture needs to be formed and ordered or searched for top-$k$. The 21-weight cache accelerates repeated offline policy assessment; it requires neither 21 full sorts at deployment nor advance knowledge of the correct entity. Even offline, an exact gold rank is computed by counting strictly greater scores, not by sorting the complete candidate list. Gold-preserving filtered RR is a separate labeled-evaluation operation (Appendix~\ref{app:inference-cache}).

\subsection{A testable meaning of conservative}
\label{sec:conservative-definition}
The policy is \emph{reference-anchored} because fallback returns exactly to a fixed DEV-selected $\alpha_0$, and \emph{bounded-action} because every applied weight satisfies $|\widehat\alpha(q)-\alpha_0|\leq\beta$ for the evaluated, grid-aligned radii. These are deterministic properties of the action rule. They do not bound the fraction of changed weights or the probability or magnitude of RR harm. We reserve claims of lower empirical harm for specified comparators and metrics.
The score identity below concerns the normalized score family in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixture}; it does not assert floating-point rank equivalence for the operational raw-endpoint dispatch.
Let $d(q,e)=\widetilde s_p(q,e)-\widetilde s_s(q,e)$. The mixture admits the identity
\begin{equation}
s_\alpha(q,e)-s_{\alpha_0}(q,e)=(\alpha-\alpha_0)d(q,e).
\label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Bounded candidate-score perturbation]
For a candidate whose two normalized scores are finite, the continuous policy in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fallback} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\left|s_{\alpha_{\mathrm{cont}}}(q,e)-s_{\alpha_0}(q,e)\right|
\leq\beta|d(q,e)|.
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
The same inequality holds after nearest-grid projection when the clipped interval endpoints $\max(0,\alpha_0-\beta)$ and $\min(1,\alpha_0+\beta)$ lie on the grid, as in the evaluated configurations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The magnitude of $\beta\tanh(g)$ is at most $\beta$. Projection onto $[0,1]$ cannot increase distance from $\alpha_0\in[0,1]$, and fallback sets that distance to zero. Grid-aligned interval endpoints ensure that the nearest grid point remains inside the interval. Applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:identity} completes the proof.
\end{proof}

This elementary property follows from the linear-mixture identity and $|\tanh(g)|\leq1$; it documents the action constraint rather than supplying a new ranking-risk theorem. The bound gives $\beta$ a direct interpretation in score space: it limits movement in proportion to inter-model disagreement. It is not an absolute, data-independent score bound, since $d$ varies with the scores. Candidate ordering also depends on the reference margins. An arbitrarily small perturbation can reverse a near tie and reduce a gold answer's RR from 1 to $1/2$. Appendix~\ref{app:bound} gives a sufficient margin condition for preserving an ordering. Without such margin information, this property supplies neither a nontrivial RR-loss bound tending to zero with $\beta$ nor a probabilistic harm guarantee. Non-finite normalized entries fall outside the finite-score algebra.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Datasets, models, and evaluation roles}
The corrected study evaluates MKG-W~\citep{mkgw_origin} and DB15K, derived from MMKG~\citep{mmkg_origin}. Our exact split and mapping files come from the NativE repository at commit \texttt{c72291c3d571}; Appendix~\ref{app:data-checkpoint} separates these files from published statistics and records their preprocessing. Earlier discovery work used OpenBG-IMG~\citep{openbg}; its numerical results are excluded here because that dataset is outside the completed boundary rerun. The four primary pairs combine M-Hyper with NativE or AdaMF-MAT on each dataset, holding the stronger architecture family constant while varying its partner. The two additional pairs combine NativE and AdaMF-MAT, with NativE selected as primary by the DEV rule, to examine transfer beyond a M-Hyper primary. These are architecture roles in the retained six-pair reporting frame, not evidence of a partition selected before first TEST inspection. We keep all six, including negative and unresolved results, and make no claim that the historical partition was independent of observed outcomes. All comparisons use three paired seeds and both head and tail prediction.

Pair codes use W for MKG-W and D for DB15K. N denotes M-Hyper + NativE, A denotes M-Hyper + AdaMF-MAT, and NA denotes NativE + AdaMF-MAT. Thus W-N, W-A, D-N and D-A are the four primary pairs; W-NA and D-NA are the two additional pairs.

MKG-W is the primary retrospective evaluation dataset. DB15K is a secondary external evaluation, also retrospective: its TEST split had already been used in earlier score-ensemble experiments. Four-pair ADC TEST results and lock hashes are recorded together in commit \texttt{f0932c2} (3 September 2026). A subsequent exposure manifest and protocol (\texttt{9a87bbe}, 4 September) explicitly record that both datasets' TEST outcomes had been inspected. \subsection{Comparison methods}
\paragraph{Standalone and static policies.}
We report both standalone models, equal query-wise z-score averaging, equal reciprocal-rank fusion (RRF, $k=60$), Global, and Relation in Table~\ref{tab:matched-static}. Relation selects a relation-specific weight on DEV, with the shared weight used for low-support relations. RRF adds $1/(60+r_p(e))+1/(60+r_s(e))$ from the complete unfiltered candidate competition ranks, then filters the combined ranking. Global is the reference for every correction metric. The expert-selection Oracle, $\max(\RR_p,\RR_s)$ per observation, appears separately in Table~\ref{tab:matched-oracle}. It uses answers, is not deployable, and is not an upper bound on score fusion: a mixture can rank the gold above both standalone experts.

\paragraph{Dynamic policies.}
Query-soft applies $\alpha(q)=p_A(q)$ using exactly the same fitted 13-feature logistic object and output preference scores as ADC, not a separately fitted classifier. This holds for each original full-geometry OOF fit and the final serialized DEV model. DynaSemble~\citep{dynasemble} uses four distribution features with query-wise min-max scores, rather than ADC's z-scores. R0 retains the historical three-seed transfer, including its failed seeds and identified sampled-training-feature and initialization-order deviations. R1 aligns full-distribution training features and initialization order with the pinned public core. R2 changes output initialization to keep the initial ReLU active. R3-fixed replaces the final ReLU with Softplus while retaining the one-epoch, $5\times10^{-5}$ learning-rate budget. R3 selects learning rate and epoch using grouped DEV cross-validation. R4 swaps the learned and fixed coefficients, inheriting R3's configuration without further tuning. Each new variant reports all nine base--selector seed combinations. Appendix~\ref{app:dynasemble} details the differences and results. These are transparent transfers to new scorer pairs, not reproductions of the original paper's datasets or model combinations.

\paragraph{Matched action alternatives.}
The supplementary comparison shares each fitted preference object, its $g,p_A$, the anchor, outer folds, normalized scores and ranking grid across six families. Shrink uses $\alpha=(1-\lambda)\alpha_0+\lambda p_A$, including $\lambda=0$ (Global) and $\lambda=1$ (Query-soft). Shrink-gate also tunes the same confidence fallback as ADC. Linear-p replaces $\tanh(g)$ by $2p_A-1$, and Clip-g replaces it by $\clip(g,-1,1)$, retaining the local radius. Linear-g uses a scaled logit with only $[0,1]$ clipping, so it does not have the same local-radius bound. Each family, including ADC + Global, receives 41 DEV configurations and explicit Global. Appendix~\ref{app:matched} specifies the grids and budget allocation; DynaSemble is not treated as a mapping-only control.

\paragraph{Component variants.}
\emph{Expanded-radius} fixes $\beta=1$ while retaining the full policy's fitted signal, $\alpha_0$, threshold, tanh mapping, $[0,1]$ clipping, grid and non-finite fallback. It expands the local radius; it is not a freely parameterized dynamic weight. At an interior anchor, the logit's sign still determines the proposal's direction and tanh still determines its magnitude before clipping and projection. \emph{No fallback} fixes $\tau=0$ while retaining $\alpha_0$, $\beta$, and the model; non-finite features still return to the reference. These variants inherit the corresponding full policy settings without additional TEST selection. Query-soft changes the complete mapping from preference to action, including the reference-relative construction and confidence fallback. Table~\ref{tab:component-design} additionally fixes the original settings while changing only proposal origin or response shape, and includes a fixed-strength linear shrinkage control. Each variant uses one configuration per original fitted object, with no added fit or parameter search; pairwise thinning compares exact expected outcomes at equal actual intervention counts (Appendix~\ref{app:component-ablation}).

\subsection{DEV fitting and TEST application}
We evaluate the combiner training procedure with five-fold grouped cross-fitting on DEV, conditional on the frozen experts, primary/secondary roles and feature family. Base-checkpoint selection is not repeated inside these folds. An original triple is the grouping unit, and all three seeds and both directions remain in one fold. Each outer training fold selects Global, fits preprocessing and logistic regression on its non-tied rows, and selects $\beta,\tau$ from the fitted preferences on that training portion. Only then is performance measured on held-out triples. This training-resubstitution choice is part of the evaluated algorithm; it does not itself use outer-holdout labels for fitting or selection. It is not an inner-CV search. Appendix~\ref{app:selection-hierarchy} compares the original procedure with a three-fold training-internal OOF sensitivity, retaining the original outer folds and TEST policies.

This is evaluation on held-out triples in a transductive query distribution, not a claim of generalization to wholly unseen query keys. Different gold answers can share $(h,r)$ for tail prediction or $(r,t)$ for head prediction across folds. Appendix~\ref{app:query-isolation} reports unique-key and answer-row overlap, and a DEV sensitivity that removes every training triple sharing a held-out query key, with a relation/size-matched removal control. Even that combiner-only purge leaves the frozen experts and their full-DEV checkpoint selection outside the folds.

After this evaluation, one classifier per pair is refitted on pooled full DEV and the final reference and policy parameters are locked. TEST applies that same serialized policy to each of the three base-checkpoint pairs and both directions without refitting. The five OOF classifiers are evaluation fits, not members of a deployment ensemble. DynaSemble instead fits separate networks per base seed, with three selector repetitions for each new control. Tables~\ref{tab:inference-method-scope}--\ref{tab:inference-fit-counts} list the fitting information, model counts and deployment units for all method families. The deployed radii are 0.45, 0.50, 0.20, and 0.50 for W-N, W-A, D-N, and D-A; thresholds are 0.1, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3. Corresponding primary-weight anchors are 0.55, 1, 1, and 1. The exact feature order and selection rules appear in Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}.
The upper limit 0.50 is a historical design restriction on the searched action family. There is no documented calibration connecting it to a tolerable RR loss or harm probability. Selection maximizes DEV MRR within that family, not over all radii. To examine the restriction, a supplementary analysis reconstructs all 20 original fold models and verifies their selected parameters and held-out predictions before comparing caps 0.50 and 1.00. Both families choose $\beta,\tau$ using training-portion MRR, with identical classifiers, anchors and tie-breaking. Held-out DEV outcomes assess the resulting trade-off. The wider family and full radius curves do not choose any new TEST policy (Appendix~\ref{app:radius-review}). This analysis follows historical TEST inspection and is descriptive.
The matched action supplement follows the same outer-fold selection procedure, then locks all 24 full-DEV family configurations before a separate TEST application phase. Within $10^{-12}$ of maximal selection MRR, it prefers explicit Global, then smaller strength and larger threshold. No family-specific learner fitting or early stopping is introduced. All candidate scores, duplicate action-vector counts, final choices and held-out results are retained. Adding Global guarantees that the best selection score is no worse than that candidate up to the tie tolerance; it does not guarantee nonnegative held-out or TEST utility.

The outer protocol separates a particular combiner fit/choice from its held-out evaluation; it does not independently evaluate the research process that produced the family. The recorded development history expands the initial radius grid and adds feature/action ablations and later matched families. OOF was part of this development, and the exact number and influence of inspection cycles are not recoverable. Neither repeated OOF use nor historical TEST exposure is corrected by adding an inner selection layer. We therefore retain retrospective, fixed-family interpretations rather than calling the current outer procedure inherently invalid or claiming that inner CV restores confirmation.

\subsection{Metrics and statistical analysis}
We report exact filtered MRR and Hits@1/3/10. For the change $\Delta_i$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta}, harm rate and benefit rate are the fractions of all evaluated observations with negative and positive changes. Conditional mean harm and benefit quantify their magnitudes:
\begin{align}
H&=N^{-1}\sum_i\ind[\Delta_i<0], & B&=N^{-1}\sum_i\ind[\Delta_i>0],\\
C&=\E[-\Delta_i\mid\Delta_i<0], & G&=\E[\Delta_i\mid\Delta_i>0].
\end{align}
These quantities yield the decomposition
\begin{equation}
U=\Delta\MRR=L_+-L_-,\qquad L_-=\E[\max(-\Delta_i,0)]=H C,\quad L_+=B G.
\label{eq:utility-harm}
\end{equation}
The unconditional mean loss $L_-$ counts every observation, including zeros, and jointly reflects harm frequency and severity. Conditional means are undefined when their event is empty; Global has $U=L_-=H=0$ and undefined $C$. For policies using the same z-score coefficient coordinate, we report weight-change frequency $I_\alpha=\Pr(\widehat\alpha\ne\alpha_0)$ and mean displacement $D_\alpha=\E|\widehat\alpha-\alpha_0|$. DynaSemble's min-max coefficient ratio cannot be subtracted directly from the z-score Global weight; its $I_\alpha,D_\alpha$ are omitted. The common outcome-change measure $J=\Pr(\Delta_i\ne0)$ is the gold-answer rank-change rate, not a full-order change rate. All probabilities and expectations here are empirical averages over the declared observations.

Let $A_i=\ind[\widehat\alpha_i\ne\alpha_{0,i}]$ denote an actual projected-weight intervention. We additionally report $H_I=\sum_i A_i\ind[\Delta_i<0]/\sum_i A_i$ with the eligible count $n_I=\sum_i A_i$, beside $H$ and $N$. Every observed harm in the replay occurs within $A$, but a changed weight need not change the gold rank. We separately report $Z=\Pr(\Delta_i=0)$, conditional gains/losses and both unconditional contributions. Ratio intervals resample full original-triple clusters before restricting to their denominator subsets and recompute each ratio; they are not ratios of interval endpoints. Appendix~\ref{app:outcome-risk} specifies complete denominators, uncertainty and predefined tail diagnostics.

The current central and complete paired TEST comparisons use one percentile clustered-bootstrap procedure: 10,000 resamples, PCG64 seeds 2026091212 for MKG-W and 2026091213 for DB15K, and the same draws for all contrasts and pairs within a dataset. Each resampling unit is a lexicographically ordered original triple containing all six base-seed--direction observations. We difference paired RR values first, average within each triple, and take linear-interpolated 2.5/97.5 percentiles of resampled means. R3 first averages reciprocal ranks across its three selector repetitions within a base-seed--direction observation. Harm rates instead use actual selector observations before averaging. The interval conditions on fitted models and observed seeds; it omits retraining, graph/query dependence beyond the triple clusters, historical TEST reuse and adaptive design uncertainty. The four primary ADC-minus-Global effects are the central scientific comparisons, but their TEST blindness was not established. All other contrasts and sensitivities are exploratory. These are pointwise intervals without multiplicity adjustment, not a familywise guarantee or independent confirmation. Zero inclusion does not establish equivalence. Appendix~\ref{app:claims-effects} gives all 82 method/component contrasts and identifies retained historical diagnostic intervals. Table~\ref{tab:selection-query-seed} additionally reports the three base-seed effects and sample SD; Tables~\ref{tab:selection-seeds-mkgw}--\ref{tab:selection-seeds-db15k} give absolute seed MRRs for all methods. Seed SD describes these observed checkpoint pairs, not a standard error or training-population interval. ADC's pooled selector is shared across seeds, and direction/pair sign counts are not independent replications.
The supplementary loss and intervention comparisons also use actual selector observations before averaging: otherwise opposite-signed changes could cancel and understate loss. The earlier cross-policy risk table compares observed point estimates. The current ADC outcome audit additionally supplies conditional intervals for its gain/loss contributions, harm denominators and predefined tail events; these are descriptive intervals, not statistical risk constraints or a guarantee of risk dominance.
Thus ADC's three base-seed results evaluate one pooled selector, not three independently fitted ADC selectors. DynaSemble's additional average combines the RR outcomes of separately fitted networks; it does not average their weights or candidate scores into a serving policy. These experiments do not estimate ADC selector-initialization uncertainty or transfer to a newly trained checkpoint pair.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why the reference matters.}
A fixed reference separates established performance from additional intervention. At the primary endpoint, direct sigmoid-score weighting can introduce substantial secondary influence. Comparisons sharing the exact fitted preference object isolate the action family from features and learner changes. They show that ADC can outperform ungated shrinkage on some pairs, while simple hard-clipped correction can match or exceed its accuracy. The fixed-setting proposal-center contrast supports a reference-centering benefit on W-A/D-N after intervention matching, but has unresolved or adverse results elsewhere (Section~\ref{sec:component-ablation}). Because fallback and projection still use the reference, this is not a complete anchor-removal experiment or a universal necessity claim. Equal configuration counts in the separately tuned families still leave different attainable action sets and different numbers of duplicate grid policies.
At $\alpha_0=1$, shrinkage with $\lambda>0$ moves below the primary endpoint whenever $p<1$, even when the signal favors the primary ($p>1/2$). In contrast, centered tanh, linear-score and clipped-logit proposals remain at that endpoint after clipping whenever $g\geq0$. This algebra distinguishes shrinkage toward the sigmoid preference score from a signed reference-relative correction; the distinction does not require tanh.

\paragraph{Preference supervision and correction utility.}
The classifier learns an endpoint-winner label, not RR improvement over Global. The label omits effect magnitude, so even correct endpoint preference does not ensure a beneficial mixture adjustment. A gold-aware Oracle also bypasses the inference problem faced by a policy using only observable score summaries and direction. Finite DEV samples can introduce estimation error, the feature representation may omit relevant distinctions, and the bounded/grid-projected action map restricts attainable mixtures. These are distinct reasons why Oracle or static complementarity need not become dynamic utility; this study does not decompose their contributions. Exploitation is assessed through actual grouped held-out DEV and locked TEST effects relative to Global, retaining their retrospective status and negative results.
The new contingency analysis makes the mismatch measurable. On W-N, 5.57\% of eligible DEV opportunities and 5.60\% on TEST have gains only opposite the endpoint winner's suggested direction. Eligibility requires a non-tied winner, some profitable point within the fixed radius and a feasible suggested direction. Among actual winner-aligned W-N TEST interventions, 7.66\% still lose RR, with conditional mean loss 0.0772 (Tables~\ref{tab:winner-contingency}--\ref{tab:winner-alignment}). These answer-aware strata explain outcomes after evaluation, not a gate available at inference. Removing class balancing lowers DEV Brier and ECE on every pair but does not uniformly improve correction utility (Table~\ref{tab:winner-weighting}); probability fit and action utility remain distinct questions.
Excluding endpoint ties from the binary loss also leaves a substantial application group without winner supervision: 17.51--24.12\% of TEST observations are tied across the six pairs. They still receive dynamic actions and can lose RR. W-N changes 79.44\% of tied TEST weights, with 48 harmed ties despite positive net tie utility. For actually executed nonzero corrections, inverse-confidence AUROC is only 0.525 on W-N; the other three primary intervals span 0.5 (Appendix~\ref{app:ties-harm}). Margin strength is neither calibrated safety confidence nor an established harm predictor.
Direct full-minus-no-fallback intervals also leave three primary TEST gate effects unresolved. Equal-count rejection controls show that quantity alone does not explain every gain, but matching direction and displacement largely reproduces the full gate, while simple gap rules sometimes perform better (Appendix~\ref{app:rejection}). The gate is a conditional policy component, not a generally necessary mechanism or an independently validated source of safety information.

\paragraph{Numerical endpoint convention.}
Exact actions 0 and 1 use shared raw standalone ranks, while interior actions use standardized mixtures. Normalization does change some endpoint gold ranks in the returned float32 audit. Substituting those ranks with actions fixed leaves every primary TEST ADC--Global RR difference unchanged within $10^{-12}$, but changes additional-pair and comparator results. One additional pair's DEV anchor also changes under the alternative endpoint grid. This supports the scoped sensitivity result, not equivalence of the two conventions or invariance after retraining (Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-review}).

\paragraph{Meaning of conservative adaptation.}
Conservative denotes the reference-anchored, bounded-action rule in Section~\ref{sec:conservative-definition}. It does not mean rare or harmless intervention: W-N changes 63.33\% of weights and harms 15.150\% of observations. The radius limits coefficient movement and the associated score perturbation; empirical ranking losses remain separate. ADC reduces selected loss measures relative to the frozen expanded-radius policy and healthy R3, but it cannot dominate Global's zero loss and does not dominate Query-soft on every W-N risk measure. The 0.50 cap restricts an MRR-optimized DEV family without an explicit risk tolerance. Wider DEV selection improves utility in three pairs at increased unconditional loss, which supplies a trade-off rather than a uniquely justified optimum. Neither this analysis nor preference confidence provides a probabilistic safety guarantee.

\paragraph{MMKGC as an application setting.}
The known-entity coverage diagnostic uses only the observed side of each query; it does not look up the unknown gold entity's modality attributes. Its heterogeneous outcomes do not support a uniform missing-modality benefit: W-A's image-only TEST group is negative while its both-supported group is positive, whereas D-N's both-supported group is negative. Availability flags do not measure semantic quality, and relation/entity composition can confound these associations. We have not performed a causal modality-removal experiment or shown that the asymmetry is uniquely multimodal. ADC therefore remains a general score-level construction evaluated on multimodal scorers, rather than a modality-specific adaptation mechanism.

\paragraph{Generalization and computation.}
The evidence covers two main evaluation datasets, three base architectures, three paired seeds, and six model pairs, with the historical OpenBG numerical results excluded from this corrected draft. The reliable-primary rule holds in all six main and additional pairs, so its necessity for successful correction remains an open question. It is not sufficient: D-N meets the rule but has slightly negative held-out DEV and TEST utility (Tables~\ref{tab:matched-dev} and~\ref{tab:claims-intervals}). A favorable endpoint gap therefore defines the studied regime, not a reason by itself to deploy ADC. Both datasets have the retrospective status described in Section~\ref{sec:setup}; DB15K is a second-dataset external evaluation, not an independent replication. Historical TEST influence on the choice of method, features and pairs cannot be ruled out by the available records. Parameter serialization, grouped DEV checks and the historical 77-check audit cannot restore holdout independence. Fresh confirmation would require a complete, traceable design freeze before inspecting an evaluation set still unexposed to that design. Inference requires both scorers even for queries that eventually fall back, since the policy uses their candidate distributions. Efficient feature extraction and score reuse are therefore important practical directions.

\section{Conclusion}
Across four primary pairs and two additional architecture pairs on MKG-W and DB15K, the retrospective evidence supports conditional gains from reference-relative correction. Three primary ADC-minus-Global conditional intervals lie above zero; D-N is slightly negative and its interval and both additional-pair intervals span zero (Table~\ref{tab:claims-intervals}). Healthy DynaSemble has higher MRR in two primary pairs, and Relation also exceeds ADC on W-N. ADC has lower unconditional RR loss relative to Global than healthy DynaSemble in the four primary pairs, while Global itself has zero such loss. The separately DEV-tuned hard-clipped correction has slightly higher MRR in all four primary pairs; fixed-setting component contrasts likewise do not establish a unique tanh advantage or universal anchor/gate necessity. The contribution is an explicit action constraint with measured, conditional trade-offs.

Anchored Dynamic Correction offers a bounded way to adjust an effective static mixture when two frozen scorers already exist and one is reliably stronger on DEV. In that setting it is worth considering only when held-out DEV gains justify the associated RR losses and the cost of obtaining both score arrays; retaining Global or choosing a stronger control remains a valid outcome. A small selector does not make dual scoring cheap: historical pre-repair timing showed substantially greater cost than primary-only, and corrected end-to-end timing remains unmeasured. The action bound is not an RR-risk guarantee. These findings do not establish benefit for pairs without a reliable primary, under distribution shift, or from modality-specific adaptation, and the retrospective design leaves independent confirmation open.

\subsection{Exact score normalization and exceptional inputs}
\label{app:normalization}
The export module is \texttt{scripts/\allowbreak eval\_\allowbreak heterogeneous\_\allowbreak complementarity.py}. Candidate construction uses \texttt{score\_expert\_block}. The unfiltered rows pass to \texttt{query\_zscore\_with\_reference}, followed by \texttt{filtered\_copy} and \texttt{endpoint\_safe\_mixed\_ranks}. The candidate output agrees with \texttt{normalize\_candidate\_scores} in \texttt{query\_zscore} mode; the separate generic \texttt{combine\_expert\_scores} helper uses a different finite-fill rule and is not this paper's ranking path.
For each model and each unfiltered row $s$, compute:
\begin{quote}\small
$f_e\leftarrow\operatorname{isfinite}(s_e)$, $n\leftarrow\max(1,\sum_e f_e)$.\\
$\mu\leftarrow\sum_e\operatorname{where}(f_e,s_e,0)/n$.\\
$v_e\leftarrow\operatorname{where}(f_e,s_e-\mu,0)$.\\
$\sigma\leftarrow\sqrt{\sum_e v_e^2/n}$, $a\leftarrow\sigma+10^{-8}$.\\
$z_e\leftarrow\operatorname{where}(f_e,v_e/a,-\infty)$.\\
Only in evaluation: $z_y^{\rm ref}\leftarrow(s_y^{\rm ref}-\mu)/a$, then filter owned candidate copies while retaining the current gold.
\end{quote}
NaN, $+\infty$ and $-\infty$ are all excluded from the moments and become $-\infty$ in $z$. The finite sets are computed independently, not intersected or united across experts. With finite moment arithmetic in the evaluated float32 dtype, one finite value or any constant finite support gives $\sigma=0$ and finite entries map to zero. With no finite values the clamped count gives $\mu=\sigma=0$ internally, but every output remains $-\infty$. The candidate normalization is row-wise and invariant to which gold or other batch rows are supplied. Arithmetic retains the incoming tensor dtype; it does not upcast moments, so sufficiently extreme finite values can overflow. Lower-precision dtypes are not covered by this numerical audit.

For an interior weight $0<\alpha<1$, the actual ranking path directly interpolates normalized copies: a candidate missing from either expert's finite support is therefore at $-\infty$. Shared answer masks also remain $-\infty$. At weights 0 and 1, the path explicitly substitutes the original expert rank, avoiding $0\cdot(-\infty)$ and preserving endpoint evaluation. A separately scored gold is transformed using the same moments, not included as an extra sample.

These rules define normalization even for degenerate rows, but they do not provide reliable abnormal-input recovery. The legacy gold transform and strict-greater rank function do not reject non-finite references: an all-non-finite row with NaN gold can return rank 1 because every comparison is false. Such output is not valid accuracy evidence. An independent test records this counterexample. The retained feature tables alone cannot certify finite raw scores, because geometry sanitization can hide non-finite scores.

A completed frozen-checkpoint re-execution audits all 18 checkpoints (two datasets, three architectures and three seeds), both DEV/TEST splits and both prediction directions: 72 cells and 474,732 expert/query scoring rows, including repetitions across models, seeds and directions. It finds zero non-finite candidate or gold scores, empty or constant finite supports, non-finite moments, or invalid normalized scores. The returned checkpoint/configuration hashes match the existing baseline records, and all four ordered splits are independently reconstructed from the bound split ledgers. The source-bound raw-score review accompanies the supplement. These findings support the finite-input conditions for this re-execution and indicate no anomaly-driven reevaluation; they do not establish bitwise equality to historical raw scores that were not retained, or robustness to arbitrary abnormal inputs. No training, policy selection or replacement of historical ranks is performed.

\paragraph{Features.}
The 13-dimensional vector is ordered as follows: tail-direction indicator; primary top-1, top-5 mean, top-1/top-2 margin, and score standard deviation; the corresponding four secondary statistics; then primary-minus-secondary differences of these four statistics. In the corrected implementation, geometry is extracted from an owned copy of the unfiltered all-entity score matrix before both masking and mixture normalization. The geometry standard deviation uses the sample correction; mixture z-score uses population variance. The feature routine sanitizes non-finite model outputs for computing statistics; evaluation-mask sentinels are not permitted inputs. The policy separately flags non-finite resulting feature vectors for fallback. Tied standalone RR labels are excluded from fitting.

\subsection{Gold and filtering boundary}
\label{app:information-boundary}
For tail prediction define $K(q)=\{e:(h,r,e)\in F\}$, and for head prediction define $K(q)=\{e:(e,r,t)\in F\}$, where $F$ is the declared evaluation fact union. Only ranking uses $K(q)\setminus\{y\}$ for the current gold $y$. A separately scored gold reference is transformed using statistics of the unfiltered candidate distribution and is never a selector input. Ranking uses $1+\sum_e\ind[s(q,e)>s(q,y)]$ (strict-greater ties); endpoint weights reuse the standalone expert ranks.

Head scoring calls each model's directional interface. M-Hyper maps $(e,r,t)$ to $(t,r^{-1},e)$ internally, with inverse relation ID $r+|\mathcal R|$ for a base relation. NativE and AdaMF-MAT directly score $(e,r,t)$. The head filter remains indexed by the original $(r,t)$ and candidate head IDs in all cases. Canonical preprocessing rejects within-split duplicate triples and exact cross-split overlaps. Filter indexes are sets, so repeated facts have no additional masking effect. The low-level reader and evaluator do not silently deduplicate observations; bypassing canonical preprocessing would retain their multiplicity.

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Inference (no gold or filter input):}\\
1. Score every entity for the observable query with both frozen experts.\\
2. Extract geometry and compute normalization statistics from the full unfiltered score vectors.\\
3. Apply the frozen selector, bound, fallback, and weight grid to obtain $\alpha(q)$.\\
4. Combine once at the selected weight (preserve the original expert at endpoints); return candidate scores/order/top-$k$.\\[3pt]
\textbf{Evaluation only:}\\
5. Copy the candidate scores; mask $K(q)\setminus\{y\}$, always retaining $y$.\\
6. Score/transform the gold reference separately and compute its strict-greater rank.\\[3pt]
\textbf{DEV supervision only:}\\
7. Form preference labels and selection metrics from DEV filtered ranks; fit preprocessing and the selector on the designated DEV training fold. TEST never refits them.
\end{quote}
Masking normalized expert copies before a fixed row-wise interpolation is equivalent to masking the combined scores for the shared filter. The copies cannot flow back into geometry or normalization. Regression tests vary the designated gold and the fact index through the actual export path and require identical geometry and policy weights for an unchanged observable query, while allowing the evaluation rank to change. The completed rerun covers six MKG-W/DB15K pairs and four retrained DynaSemble transfers. Across all six pairs, deployed ADC and Query-soft grid weights are exactly equal within every multi-gold observable-query group in both DEV and TEST. Some exported geometry and continuous outputs differ slightly across repeated observations: the largest ADC continuous-weight span is $4.21\times10^{-7}$ and the largest DynaSemble learned-weight span is $2.39\times10^{-7}$. These observations are reported separately from exact grid-weight invariance. Controlled gold/filter intervention tests pass, but the exports alone do not establish the cause of these numerical differences or bitwise repeatability under arbitrary batching. No post-hoc averaging across golds was applied.

\subsection{Offline rank caches and real inference}
\label{app:inference-cache}
The experiment exports align each original-triple, base-seed and direction row with 13 observable features, standalone RR values and 21 mixture RR values, one per grid weight. Gold IDs and separately scored gold references support labeled evaluation; none enters the feature-to-weight function. DEV standalone RR values define winner labels, while the grid RR values permit selection of anchors and action parameters without rescoring every candidate for every policy. On TEST, the locked policy first produces its weight from features; only then does the assessment code read that weight's cached RR. A gold-rank cache stores the outcome needed to assess a labeled observation, not a candidate ordering that a new query must possess.

This separation is explicit in the code paths. In \path{scripts/crossfit_anchored_dynamic.py}, \texttt{apply\_policy} computes the gate, proposal and projected weight before reading the selected RR column. The historical lock/apply experiment CLI also requires RR and metadata columns because it emits evaluation summaries; it is not a serving interface. The guarded \texttt{apply\_frame} in \path{scripts/apply_anchored_safe.py} requires only the 13 feature columns and the frozen model/lock, returning weights and validity diagnostics. Optional query/seed metadata are copied for joining and do not affect predictions. Six feature-only tests exercise absent or corrupted gold/RR/seed metadata, individual versus batched application on finite features, and rejection of a missing observable feature. This helper does not load the two base models or implement a complete retrieval service.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:policy} describes that retrieval composition: score the observable query with the two frozen checkpoints, extract geometry, choose one weight, and form its candidate-score vector. Sorting or top-$k$ selection then uses that vector. Offline grid export may make 21 score-comparison passes to obtain exact gold ranks, but strict-greater counting requires no full sort. Inference does not compute filtered RR, needs no designated correct entity and does not enumerate all 21 mixtures. It still incurs both scorers' candidate scoring and geometry extraction, including queries ultimately assigned the anchor; this distinction does not establish low end-to-end latency.

\paragraph{Classifier.}
The implementation uses median imputation, feature standardization, and \texttt{LogisticRegression} with \texttt{class\_weight=balanced}, \texttt{solver=liblinear}, \texttt{max\_iter=2000}, and default $C=1$. The final model random state is 20260902; outer-fold fits use their recorded deterministic offsets. Fitted imputation and scaling statistics are reused on held-out and TEST observations. Confidence is the absolute distance of the sigmoid preference score from 0.5, scaled to $[0,1]$.

The geometry standard-deviation coordinate uses \texttt{torch.std} with sample correction 1, unlike population normalization above. Top-5 means use $\min(5,|\mathcal E|)$ candidates; geometry requires at least two candidates. Its existing sanitizer uses the finite minimum/maximum over the entire supplied expert batch, with low=min$-1$ and high=max$+1$, or $-100,100$ when the batch has no finite values; NaN/$-\infty$ become low and $+\infty$ becomes high. Thus abnormal geometry can depend on other batch rows even though normalization does not. The feature guard cannot detect raw anomalies already sanitized upstream, so it does not establish batch invariance or robust ranking for arbitrary abnormal raw scores.

\paragraph{Guarded application and historical replay.}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:policy} reuses the original model and lock without fitting. Its entry point is \path{scripts/apply_anchored_safe.py}. Non-finite feature rows bypass preprocessing entirely; transformed overflow is removed before classification. Non-finite logits and invalid sigmoid scores also retain the anchor, including at $\tau=0$. Exported validity flags distinguish skipped rows from predictions; neutral diagnostic placeholders are not inferred preferences. Schema errors or corrupt fitted state remain explicit errors. The hash-bound historical experiment scripts retain their original call order for reproduction: remaining non-finite coordinates were explicitly changed to NaN, imputed, predicted and then assigned fallback. That earlier order can fail when another large coordinate overflows during scaling; median imputation alone does not prevent this failure.

The guarded entry point reproduces ADC/Query-soft grid weights and fallback flags exactly on all 474,732 saved DEV/TEST observations across six pairs, with no rejected rows. Margins, sigmoid scores, continuous ADC weights and cached RR agree within absolute $10^{-12}$. Full-DEV and OOF training-portion anchor selection reproduces all 36 recorded anchors. This CPU replay uses the original fitted models, makes no TEST selection and changes no historical result. It verifies the guard on observed features, not arbitrary raw-score sanitization; synthetic tests separately enforce the before-call rejection order.

\paragraph{Grouping and selection.}
Five-fold assignments use deterministic relation-stratified SHA-256 ordering and round-robin allocation of original triples, with fold seed 20260901. The six observations of a triple share a fold. The outer training data supply the static anchor, classifier, and training-portion MRR used to select the bounded policy. This is selection within each outer training fold; it does not introduce an additional inner cross-validation loop. The final full-DEV fit selects from the same expanded grids and serializes all parameters and source hashes.

\paragraph{Grid application and endpoints.}
\label{app:endpoint-contract}
Continuous weights are mapped to the closest element of $\Agrid$. Ties use the lexicographic order of distance to the proposed value, distance to $\alpha_0$, and weight value. At $\alpha=0$ or 1, the evaluator uses the corresponding exact standalone endpoint rank. The candidate set remains the full entity universe throughout. Relation weights use a minimum support of 60 DEV observations, with low-support relations assigned the Global weight.

Standalone ranking uses the shared evaluator's strict-greater rule, $1+\#\{e:s(q,e)>s(q,y)\}$ after gold-preserving filtering; equal scores do not increase rank. Tests compare both endpoint branches row by row with the actual shared head/tail evaluator, including score ties, constant rows, non-finite candidates, and an invalid inactive expert. Dense and sparse filter paths agree on these cases. These are checks of the deployed convention, not proof that a normalized endpoint always has the same rank.

In exact arithmetic, finite scores and a common finite positive denominator in Eq.~\eqref{eq:zscore} preserve strict comparisons and ties, including constant rows. This implication can fail in floating point: for float32 scores $[0,1,10^8]$ and the first entry as gold, normalization merges the first two values, changing rank 3 to 2. Active non-finite scores also preclude unconditional equivalence; the inactive expert must be ignored at an endpoint to avoid contamination through $0\times\infty$. Consequently we state the endpoint dispatch explicitly rather than assert continuity of ranking utility at the boundary.

\section{Endpoint Complementarity and Observable Query Conditions}
\label{app:complementarity}

\paragraph{Definitions and inference boundary.}
For observation $i$, let $a_i=\RR_{A,i}$ and $b_i=\RR_{B,i}$, with A the previously designated DEV primary. Endpoint winner fractions are the empirical frequencies of $a_i>b_i$, $b_i>a_i$ and equality. We report
\begin{equation}
O=\E[\max(a_i,b_i)]-\max\{\E[a_i],\E[b_i]\},\qquad
D_r=\E\left[\left|\log_2\frac{r_{A,i}}{r_{B,i}}\right|\right].
\label{eq:complementarity}
\end{equation}
The best single is chosen once for the displayed population, whereas the diagnostic Oracle uses the per-observation better endpoint. The identity $\E[\max(a_i,b_i)]-\E[a_i]=\E[(b_i-a_i)_+]$ relates endpoint opportunity to the magnitude of secondary wins. Neither this Oracle nor its gap bounds all score mixtures (Appendix~\ref{app:matched}). $D_r$ and gold Hits@10 disagreement concern the designated gold's two ranks, not a full candidate-order distance. They and endpoint winners are answer-aware evaluation statistics and never enter the selector.

For a partition into groups $g$, define $U_g=\E[\Delta_i\mid g]$ and $C_g=\Pr(g)U_g$. The audit checks $\sum_g C_g=\E[\Delta_i]$ independently for winners, rank-ratio bins, direction and observable support. Conditional means are undefined for empty groups, whose contributions are zero. Counts distinguish seed-direction observations, original triples and directional triple queries with seeds collapsed. DEV utility uses the existing grouped held-out ADC and Global outputs, joined one-to-one to endpoint exports; TEST utility uses the existing complete locked policies. There is no refitting, parameter search, significance test or new pair selection.

\section{Winner-Label Mismatch and Preference-Score Semantics}
\label{app:winner-signal}

\subsection{Endpoint winner versus profitable correction}
Let $R_i(a)$ denote the cached filtered RR at weight $a$, and retain the original policy's anchor $a_{0i}$ and radius $\beta_i$. For DEV these are the training-fold selections applied to held-out triples; TEST uses the original full-DEV locks. Define
\begin{align}
G_i^-&=\max_{a\in\Agrid:\ a<a_{0i},\ |a-a_{0i}|\leq\beta_i}\{R_i(a)-R_i(a_{0i})\},\\
G_i^+&=\max_{a\in\Agrid:\ a>a_{0i},\ |a-a_{0i}|\leq\beta_i}\{R_i(a)-R_i(a_{0i})\}.
\label{eq:winner-direction}
\end{align}
An empty side is unavailable. A side is profitable only if its maximum exceeds $10^{-12}$; zero change is not a gain. Table~\ref{tab:winner-contingency} retains down-only, up-only, both and neither, separately for A wins, B wins and ties on all six pairs. These are gold-aware existence diagnostics over the fixed action grid, not newly fitted labels, deployable choices, a smooth derivative or a bound on all score fusion. They retain the reported raw standalone endpoint dispatch and its numerical limitations.

\input{tables/winner_signal/contingency.tex}

The endpoint label suggests moving up for A wins and down for B wins. In W-N, where the reference is interior, 889 of 15,974 eligible DEV opportunity observations (5.57\%) and 883 of 15,780 on TEST (5.60\%) have a profitable point only on the opposite side. W-NA has larger corresponding mismatch, 14.66\% and 14.82\%. Eligibility requires a non-tied label, at least one profitable side and a feasible label-suggested side; it excludes blocked endpoint suggestions. The zero miss rates for W-A, D-N, D-A and D-NA are structural under their primary-endpoint anchors: only the downward direction is feasible, so every eligible observation already has a profitable downward point. They do not establish perfect directional prediction. The blocked-opportunity counts separately expose cases where A wins but some lower mixture weight improves the primary anchor; for example, W-A TEST has 3,928 such observations.

\input{tables/winner_signal/alignment.tex}

Existence of a profitable point still does not validate the chosen magnitude. Among 6,806 actual winner-aligned W-N TEST interventions, 66.34\% benefit and 7.66\% harm; harmful cases lose 0.0772 RR on average. Their mean utility is positive (0.02095), showing that the signal can be useful conditionally while remaining imperfect. These aligned actions contribute $+0.005561$ to all-query W-N utility, whereas 4,520 winner-opposed interventions contribute $-0.003195$; tied-endpoint interventions contribute $+0.000081$ and unchanged actions zero. The sum is the reported $+0.002447$ overall effect. Conditioning on gold alignment cannot be used to select deployments. In particular, 3,555 W-N TEST observations have some profitable point on their suggested side but no positive immediate step; best-in-radius opportunity is not local monotonicity. No new policy or TEST parameter is chosen by these diagnostics.

\subsection{Balanced scores, natural winner frequencies and harm}
In a non-tied training subset of size $n$ with class counts $n_0,n_1$, the logistic loss uses $w_c=n/(2n_c)$ for \texttt{class\_weight=balanced}.\footnote{The weighting rule is specified in the \href{https://scikit-learn.org/1.7/modules/generated/sklearn.linear_model.LogisticRegression.html}{scikit-learn 1.7 LogisticRegression documentation}.} To distinguish the estimands, write $\pi(x)=P(\RR_A>\RR_B\mid x,\RR_A\ne\RR_B)$ for the natural non-tied winner probability. An ideal unrestricted minimizer of weighted binary log loss, with fixed positive class weights and no regularization, would be
\begin{equation}
q^*(x)=\frac{w_1\pi(x)}{w_1\pi(x)+w_0[1-\pi(x)]}.
\label{eq:weighted-preference}
\end{equation}
This algebra does not say the fitted regularized logistic model attains $q^*$ or supplies calibrated probabilities. It explains why its sigmoid output should not automatically be identified with $\pi$. Moreover, $\pi$ excludes endpoint ties; it is not an unconditional win probability over all evaluated queries. The probability of harm under an action, $P(R_i(a)<R_i(a_{0i})\mid x)$, is a third, action-dependent quantity. Neither $p_A$, $1-p_A$ nor $|2p_A-1|=|\tanh(g/2)|$ estimates it in this study. ADC uses the margin and its sigmoid transform as preference scores, with DEV-MRR-selected action parameters.

To measure the effect of balancing, the DEV-only control reconstructs the five original full-geometry fits per pair and fits an otherwise identical unweighted logistic model. Both use the same pooled non-tied observations, imputation/scaling, $C=1$, solver, maximum iterations and random states. All original balanced ADC weights, RR values, Query-soft RR values and anchors are replayed on 219,564 held-out observations. Preprocessing matches between weighting choices. The 30 balanced reconstructions and 30 new unweighted fits run on one CPU thread and converge within seven iterations; there is no base-model scoring. Each learner independently selects from the same 41 ADC+Global candidates using training-portion MRR, including explicit Global, for 2,460 candidate evaluations in total. Query-soft uses that learner's same object without further tuning. There is no final full-DEV control fit, new TEST application, post-hoc calibration or selection of the better weighting scheme for the main results.

\input{tables/winner_signal/weighting.tex}

For probability assessment, held-out endpoint ties are excluded and the remaining observations receive equal weight, retaining their empirical class prevalence. Brier, log loss, ROC AUC and ten equal-width-bin ECE assess this specified winner-label population; policy utility, loss and intervention rates instead include every held-out observation. ECE uses bins $[0,0.1),\ldots,[0.9,1]$ and preserves empty-bin records. Brier and log loss combine calibration and discrimination, while ECE is a bin-dependent summary, not a certificate of calibration. The training-fold prior baseline has near-zero pooled ECE despite supplying no within-fold query discrimination, illustrating why a small ECE alone is insufficient.

Unweighted fitting lowers Brier and ECE on all six pairs. ECE drops from 1.39--14.16\% with balancing to 0.23--1.63\%, while endpoint-winner AUC remains modest (approximately 0.53--0.60). Better agreement with natural winner frequencies does not uniformly improve action utility. On W-N, unweighted ADC+Global raises OOF utility from 0.00307 to 0.00368 but raises unconditional mean loss from 0.00470 to 0.00674 and intervention from 63.29\% to 82.85\%. It reduces utility on W-A and D-A, and selects Global in every W-NA fold, giving zero utility and loss. D-N remains slightly negative under either fit. Query-soft improves relative to its balanced version on every pair but remains negative relative to Global on five of six. These outcomes support separating preference-score quality, natural winner probability and action-dependent harm; they do not justify adopting a new score calibration or a new TEST policy.

\section{Matched Rejection Controls and Paired Gate Effects}
\label{app:rejection}

\paragraph{Fixed proposals and observable query units.}
The original OOF DEV and locked TEST states supply the ungated projected proposal in Eq.~\eqref{eq:harm-raw-proposal}. Only nonzero proposals enter the retention pool; clipping to an endpoint and the projection dead zone have already been applied. A retained proposal keeps that exact weight, while rejection returns to the corresponding anchor. We replay all 474,732 original observations, with no selector fitting or scorer execution.

One rejection unit is the observable tuple (base seed, fitted fold, direction, relation, known entity). TEST has one final fitted object per pair; DEV preserves each original held-out fold. Repeated requests for the same observable query under the same object share one action, even when the evaluation records specify different gold answers. Replacing those golds does not change the unit map or allocations. Each unit's repetition count is computed from observable request keys, without inspecting answers or RRs.

For exact intervention matching, form strata by base seed, fitted fold and observable-query repetition count. If a stratum has $n_s$ eligible units and full ADC retains $k_s$, every control retains exactly $k_s$ whole units. Equal repetition counts within a stratum ensure the same observation-level nonzero intervention count, including within seed/fold, in every realized gate. This is actual post-clipping/projected movement, not nominal confidence coverage. The quotas use only full's gold-free actions. They condition on the observed batch and original policy's budget, so these are diagnostic allocations rather than independently selected streaming policies.

\paragraph{Fixed simple rules and random rejection.}
Four deterministic priorities are frozen and all reported, with no DEV or TEST winner selection. Head-first and Tail-first rank only by direction. Define the unfiltered normalized gap $m_j=(s_{j,(1)}-s_{j,(2)})/\max(\sigma_j,10^{-12})$, using the exported population score standard deviation. Low-A-gap ranks by $-m_A$. Gap-agree ranks by $\operatorname{sign}(\alpha^{\rm raw}-\alpha_0)(m_A-m_B)$, favoring the proposed direction when that expert has a larger gap. Ties use the same fixed pseudorandom ordering of canonically sorted observable keys. The rules inspect no gold, winner label or evaluation outcome and have no fitted parameters or new hyperparameter search.

Random-I uniformly samples $k_s$ units without replacement in each stratum. Every draw has the same intervention count as full. Reported random-row utility, harm and loss are exact expectations: each unit's raw-action outcome receives retention probability $k_s/n_s$. They are expectations of randomized rejection, not ranks computed at an average mixture weight. Random-shape additionally stratifies by direction and signed projected displacement in 0.05 steps. Every draw then matches full's direction-by-displacement histogram and mean absolute movement, as well as intervention count, within each seed/fold. This jointly controls feasible directions, clipping and amplitude distribution. In strata with $k_s=0$ or $k_s=n_s$ the allocation is deterministic.

\paragraph{Metrics and uncertainty.}
Tables~\ref{tab:rejection-dev_oof}--\ref{tab:rejection-test} use the original observation weighting and the same anchor reference for every gate. Each reports actual intervention $I$, mean movement $D$, net utility $U$, harm frequency $H$ and unconditional mean loss $L_-$. No-fallback has different intervention counts and appears separately in Table~\ref{tab:rejection-fallback}, with direct paired full-minus-no-fallback intervals. The supplement retains all 576 method/pair/split/seed/direction cells, including 72 direct no-fallback contrasts.

Paired differences are computed observation by observation before 2,000 original-triple bootstrap resamples, preserving all three seeds and both directions. Intervals condition on fitted models, fold assignments and fixed observed-workload allocations/quotas; controls are not reallocated during resampling. They omit fitting, selection and new-workload uncertainty and are not adjusted for multiple comparisons. For randomized controls, the paired interval compares full with the exact random expectation. Table~\ref{tab:rejection-random} separately reports the 2.5/97.5 percentiles of 2,000 random gate realizations on the fixed workload. Those ranges describe randomness of rejection identities, not population confidence intervals; no draw is selected.

\section{Corrected Coverage, Aggregation and AP Definitions}
\label{app:coverage-quality}

\paragraph{Scope and gold-free allocation.}
The \path{coverage_quality_v1} analysis replays 474,732 corrected observations with their original OOF or full-DEV states. No scorer, fitting, threshold search or new policy selection is performed. \paragraph{Average precision, not trapezoidal PR area.}
At distinct score thresholds in descending order, let $P_k,R_k$ be precision and recall after including the entire tied-score group, with $R_0=0$. Both the historical confidence script and the current \path{RankedBinary.evaluate} compute
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AP}=\sum_k (R_k-R_{k-1})P_k,\qquad
\mathrm{AP\ lift}=\mathrm{AP}-P_h.
\label{eq:coverage-ap}
\end{equation}
This is non-interpolated average precision, not trapezoidal precision--recall area. The historical CSV fields \path{auprc_risk_to_harm} and \path{auprc_lift_over_prevalence} map to AP and AP-minus-prevalence, respectively; their archived bytes are unchanged. Formal tables use AP. Lift in percentage points is an absolute difference, not a ratio or relative percent increase. The two implementations and scikit-learn 1.7.1's \path{average_precision_score} agree on all 36 corrected pair/split/population points within $5.6\times10^{-17}$. Existing per-pair intervals recompute AP minus each bootstrap sample's own prevalence.

Macro AP is the mean of four pair APs. Pooled (micro) AP concatenates their original scores and labels and recomputes a single threshold ranking; it cannot be recovered as a size-weighted mean of pair APs. Pooled prevalence uses the same population counts; macro prevalence is their equal-pair mean. Pooling uncalibrated preference scores introduces cross-pair comparisons, so aggregate AP is descriptive rather than evidence of a calibrated cross-pair harm predictor. \section{Additional Bounds and Diagnostic Definitions}
\label{app:bound}
For two candidates $e_1,e_2$ with finite normalized scores from both experts, define the reference margin
\begin{equation}
m_0=s_{\alpha_0}(q,e_1)-s_{\alpha_0}(q,e_2).
\end{equation}
The change in this margin is $(\alpha-\alpha_0)[d(q,e_1)-d(q,e_2)]$. Consequently, if
\begin{equation}
m_0>\beta|d(q,e_1)-d(q,e_2)|,
\label{eq:margin-sufficient}
\end{equation}
the ordering $e_1\succ e_2$ is preserved for the evaluated bounded policy. This is a sufficient certificate, not a necessary condition: failing it does not imply that the realized correction changes the order. To certify preservation of the reference top candidate by this argument, it suffices to check the inequality against every alternative; this does not certify the rest of the ranking. Preserving every strict pairwise order requires the corresponding conditions for all such pairs. No claim is made that these conditions hold for every query. For grids whose interval endpoints are not aligned, nearest-grid projection adds at most half the grid spacing to the distance bound; the present spacing is 0.05.

\paragraph{Limits of the bound.}
A regression test constructs normalized score vectors with a near-tied gold and competitor, then verifies that a weight displacement of $10^{-6}$ respects the score bound yet changes gold RR from 1 to $1/2$. Separate tests match grid projection against the production tie rule, verify Global's zero unconditional loss, and demonstrate why selector repetitions must not be averaged before loss calculation. These check action semantics and metric arithmetic; they do not certify population ranking risk.

\paragraph{Fallback accounting.}
On a query assigned to confidence fallback, compare the raw bounded RR with Global. A lower raw RR contributes to avoided loss, a higher one contributes to sacrificed gain, and an equal one is neutral. The net utility is
\begin{equation}
U_{\mathrm{fb}}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal F}(\RR_{\alpha_0,i}-\RR_{\mathrm{raw},i}),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal F$ is the confidence-fallback subset. $U_{\mathrm{fb}}/N$ is the full-minus-no-fallback MRR difference. Non-finite fallback remains active in both policies.

\section{Redundant Difference Coordinates and Matched Dimensions}
\label{app:feature-dimension}
Let $x_9$ contain direction and the eight per-expert statistics. In exact arithmetic on complete inputs, $x_{13}=Dx_9$, where $D$ stacks the $9\times9$ identity and four rows computing the corresponding A-minus-B statistics. Thus $w_{13}^{\top}x_{13}+b=(D^{\top}w_{13})^{\top}x_9+b$: the added coordinates do not enlarge the affine function class. After column standardization, write $[z_{13};1]=M[z_9;1]$, including the centering offset. Since $M$ has full column rank, the minimal squared coefficient norm representing an effective augmented 9D coefficient $v$ is
\begin{equation}
\min_{M^{\top}w=v}\|w\|_2^2=v^{\top}(M^{\top}M)^{-1}v.
\label{eq:redundant-penalty}
\end{equation}
This differs from isotropic 9D L2 regularization. The augmented expression includes the unit synthetic intercept penalized by the evaluated liblinear configuration. As a simple unscaled example, features $(a,b,a-b)$ induce a minimum penalty $(2v_a^2+2v_av_b+2v_b^2)/3$, rather than $v_a^2+v_b^2$. These identities explain a possible parameterization effect; they do not attribute every observed MRR difference solely to regularization.

Column-wise median imputation can break the exact dependency: medians of $a,b,a-b$ need not satisfy the subtraction identity. Remaining non-finite input coordinates are imputed during fitting and flagged for fallback during inference. In the four-pair cache used here, all exported 13D DEV/TEST rows are finite, so imputation does not create the observed differences. Stored float32 subtractions leave residuals up to $1.91\times10^{-6}$; collapsing fitted 13D classifiers to nine raw affine coordinates changes audited decisions by at most $3.17\times10^{-7}$. This numerical residue is not evidence of four independent signals, nor does finite exported geometry certify finite raw scores.

Both dimensions use the same median/scaling pipeline, balanced winner-label logistic loss, $C=1$, liblinear solver, maximum 2,000 iterations, training examples, five grouped DEV folds and random states. Each dimension searches the same 41 ADC configurations, including Global; Query-soft uses that dimension's fitted model without extra tuning. All eight full-DEV pair/dimension locks precede the separate TEST application. The experiment uses 44 small model fits and four reused original full-DEV models; convergence takes at most seven iterations. The runtime is scikit-learn 1.7.1 and the original pickles are 1.7.2. Separate DEV-only refits quantify their small numerical differences and reproduce every one of the 42 DEV action vectors (41 ADC candidates plus Query-soft); the original full-DEV/TEST outputs and grouped OOF ADC ranks are also replayed. This documents numerical compatibility without a bitwise cross-version claim.
